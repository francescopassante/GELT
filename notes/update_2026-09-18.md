## 1. The baseline that was missing: a matched-parameter L-CNN

**The question an examiner asks first**, and which the thesis had never answered:
is the advantage *attention*, or would any gauge-equivariant network trained on
the same inputs with the same loss do the same? The natural baseline is the
L-CNN of Favoni et al. (2012.12901), whose primitives GELT is built on.

It is implemented as a **switch inside `train_glueball.py`** (`GLUEBALL_ARCH=lcnn`),
not a sibling script: ensemble, cache key, splits, input channels, Rayleigh loss,
checkpoint selection, jackknife and dump are literally the same code, so the
comparison cannot silently stop being one. "Matched" means matched on four axes,
not just parameter count:

| axis | GELT | L-CNN |
|---|---|---|
| parameters (real DOFs) | 15 693 (d_model 16, 4 levels) | 14 305 at `c_hidden = 5` — 0.91× |
| receptive field | R = 2 L1-ball × 4 layers → Manhattan 8 | K = 2 symmetric × 4 layers → Manhattan 8 |
| loop degree | bilinear value path doubles per layer → ≤ 16 | L-CB doubles per layer → ≤ 16 |
| inputs | 3 spatial-plaquette channels per APE level | identical |

The one remaining asymmetry is deliberate and is the thing under test: L-Conv's
steps are axis-aligned, GELT's transport is shortest-path-averaged over the whole
L1-ball. **Parity was pre-registered before the run.**

### Result — parity, as pre-registered

Same 400 held-out configurations, fit window Δ ∈ [2,7], GEVP (t0,td) = (1,2),
blocked jackknife; the two learned operators are differenced **inside every
jackknife sample** (the two ΔA₀-vs-GEVP numbers must not be subtracted by hand —
they are correlated through the classical arm, and the correlated error is ~4×
smaller).

| | ens0 | ens1 | combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| A₀(GELT) | 0.903 ± 0.047 | 1.013 ± 0.062 | |
| A₀(L-CNN) | 0.896 ± 0.047 | 0.997 ± 0.062 | |
| A₀(GEVP-projected) | 0.837 ± 0.056 | 0.925 ± 0.071 | |
| ΔA₀(GELT − GEVP) | +0.066 ± 0.031 | +0.089 ± 0.030 | **+0.077 ± 0.022** (3.6σ) |
| ΔA₀(L-CNN − GEVP) | +0.058 ± 0.031 | +0.072 ± 0.026 | **+0.067 ± 0.020** (3.4σ) |
| **ΔA₀(GELT − L-CNN)** | +0.007 ± 0.013 | +0.016 ± 0.013 | **+0.012 ± 0.009** (1.3σ) |
| Δm(GELT − L-CNN) | +0.0019 ± 0.0052 | +0.0016 ± 0.0060 | consistent with 0 |

Parity extends to the error bars: blocked jackknife of δC(Δ)/C(Δ) on the same
configurations gives 0.0322 / 0.0396 / 0.0720 / 0.1695 for GELT at Δ = 1/2/4/6
against 0.0322 / 0.0399 / 0.0728 / 0.1676 for the L-CNN — indistinguishable.

### The one asymmetry that parity does not contain: robustness

Diagnosing a failed L-CNN run turned into a result. **A single test configuration
carried 71.8% of that operator's C(0)** — 1187× the median configuration — which
destroys A₀ (a ratio to C(0)) while leaving the mass fine. That configuration is
unremarkable to every classical operator (rank 145/400 by variance at thin
links), so it is the architecture, not the configuration. Counted over every
learned operator on disk today (the right panel above):

| operator family | above the 10% gate | worst |
|---|---|---|
| GELT (8 trained + 18 untrained) | **0 of 26** | 1.0% |
| L-CNN (7 trained + 6 untrained) | **3 of 13** | 97.9% |

The mechanism is not subtle: an L-CB stack is a raw matrix polynomial (degree
≤ 16 after four layers) aggregated by L-Conv with **unbounded** weights, so a
configuration in the tail of the input distribution is amplified without limit.
GEMHSA aggregates with a **softmax** — a convex combination with weights in
(0,1) on a score already normalised by √(d_qkv·n_c). It *cannot* put 72% of its
output variance on one configuration.

Two honesty notes, both enforced in code: `fit_glueball_overlap.py` now prints
the top-1 share for **every** arm and refuses to quote an A₀ above 10%, uniformly
— so it is a gate, not something applied to an arm one dislikes; and the bad run
was re-run rather than having the offending configuration deleted (the published
protocol has no outlier rejection). This is a **failure rate across training
runs**, not an error-bar advantage — see the δC/C parity above.

### What this changes in the write-up

* The general claim gets **stronger**: "a learned gauge-equivariant operator
  beats the classical multi-level GEVP" is not an artefact of attention.
* The attention-specific chapter is untouched — the L-CNN has no attention map,
  so "the attention field is itself a measurable lattice operator" is not a claim
  parity can dent.
* The architecture chapter must now say what attention buys **precisely**: the
  L1-ball receptive field *within* a layer, an attention field that is a
  measurable operator, robustness across training runs — and **3.9× the step
  cost** (5.04 s vs 1.29 s on the V100 at production shape). Not raw overlap.

## 2. Audit 1 — is the classical comparator a straw man?

The published comparator is the GEVP over four APE smearing levels of the 1×1
plaquette. A referee will ask whether a lattice practitioner would have used
something stronger. Four strengthened arms were built on the *same*
configurations and the same estimator: `deep` (7 smearing levels), `shapes`
(thin links, 5 rectangular loop shapes), `shapes_sm` (4 levels × 5 shapes = 20
operators) and `full` (7 levels × 3 shapes = 21 operators).

One estimator repair was needed first: under an eigenvalue **floor** the 21-operator
C(t0) has cond = ∞ and `full` is unreadable. Dropping the near-null directions
instead of flooring them (`truncate=True`) makes it readable and moves `published`
by nothing (identical to three decimals), so it is not a change that favours us.

![Fair fight forest plot](update_2026-09-18/fair_fight_forest.png)

*Both ensembles inverse-variance combined, against the 7-level GELT. Red = the
arm's GEVP fell back to its best single member on ens1.*

| arm | n_ops | A₀ (combined) | ΔA₀ = GELT(7lv) − arm |
|---|---|---|---|
| thin | 1 | 0.407 ± 0.054 | +0.569 ± 0.069 |
| published (4 levels) | 4 | 0.870 ± 0.044 | +0.131 ± 0.029 (4.5σ) |
| **deep (7 levels)** | 7 | 0.921 ± 0.045 | **+0.095 ± 0.021 (4.5σ)** |
| shapes | 5 | 0.556 ± 0.078 | +0.454 ± 0.084 ⚠ |
| shapes_sm | 20 | 0.983 ± 0.049 | +0.059 ± 0.029 ⚠ |
| **full (7 levels + shapes)** | 21 | 0.948 ± 0.042 | **+0.038 ± 0.027 (1.4σ)** |

**Verdict: the claim survives, and the question "does real loop-shape variety
close the gap?" now has an answer.** Loop shapes on top of the deep ladder close
about **60%** of the gap and leave a 1–2σ GELT edge — which is exactly the
pre-registered expectation ("parity within a few hundredths, or a 1–2σ edge").
Against the input-matched `deep` arm the spectroscopy claim is unchanged at
4.5σ.

Two caveats I would state out loud in the thesis:

1. ⚠ `shapes` and `shapes_sm` fail the variational gate on ens1 under *every*
   estimator setting, and the cause is not conditioning: the GEVP at t0 = 1
   maximises C(2)/C(1), while the gate tests C(2)/C(0), and nothing guarantees
   the second at t0 ≠ 0. The projection buys its 1→2 decay with a large Δ = 0
   piece. No whitening change can rescue those two arms; they are reported with
   the fallback flagged rather than dropped.
2. `deep` and `full` both sit at A₀ ≥ 0.90, so this verdict is read off a
   **compressed observable** — above four smearing levels A₀ cannot separate the
   methods much further.

<a name="3"></a>
## 3. Is the advantage the architecture, or just richer inputs?

The cleanest version of the same worry: as the input content handed to *both*
methods grows, what happens to the gap? Three traces — classical GEVP, trained
GELT, untrained GELT — at three input depths (thin links, 4 levels, 7 levels),
on both ensembles, at one fixed width.

![Input–architecture curve](update_2026-09-18/input_architecture_curve.png)

| x | classical | trained | untrained | ΔA₀ (correlated) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| thin | 0.407 ± 0.054 | 0.680 ± 0.053 | 0.257 ± 0.054 | **+0.252 ± 0.056** (4.5σ) |
| 4 levels | 0.870 ± 0.044 | 0.949 ± 0.039 | 0.512 ± 0.131 | **+0.075 ± 0.019** (3.9σ) |
| 7 levels | 0.921 ± 0.045 | 1.008 ± 0.041 | 0.547 ± 0.136 | +0.095 ± 0.021 (saturated) |

Four readings, of which two are negative and are reported as such:

* **The advantage is real at every depth the observable can resolve**, and it
  **shrinks** as the classical side is fed more: +0.25 at thin links → +0.08 at
  four levels. It is *not* an additive constant (a constant fit gives
  χ²/dof = 9.1).
* **At four levels one learned operator is worth a whole extra smearing rung**:
  GELT(4lv) − GEVP(7lv) = +0.038 ± 0.024. At thin links it is not
  (−0.201 ± 0.048) — the inputs matter, and the network cannot manufacture
  smearing it was never given.
* **The untrained trace is below the classical GEVP at every depth**
  (0.257 / 0.512 / 0.547 against 0.407 / 0.870 / 0.921), so the advantage is
  attributable to *training*, not to the equivariant architecture alone. This is
  the control the operator-decomposition study had asked for, now at three input
  depths.
* **Capacity is not the explanation.** d_model 16 → 24 changes A₀ by nothing
  (0.903 vs 0.911 on ens0; 1.013 vs 1.002 on ens1), so the 4lv → 7lv gain is
  input content, not width. That closes a confound the first audit had flagged.

<a name="4"></a>
## 4. Audit 2 — what did the network actually find?

Write `O_GELT = P + r`, with `P` the orthogonal projection onto the classical
span in the exact Hilbert-space metric `C_ab(0)` and `r` the remainder. This is
an exact decomposition, not a fit, and both pieces are measurable operators.

![Operator decomposition](update_2026-09-18/operator_decomposition_deep_7lv.png)

*Left: new operator content per addition — each classical rung adds
exponentially less, and the learned operator (★) sits at 12.8%, an order of
magnitude above where the ladder had converged. Right: ρ(Δ) of each piece — `r`
alone is a poor operator (A₀ ≈ 0.43); it wins by constructive interference with
`P`.*

**The same net against three nested spans** (7-level net, both ensembles):

| span | n_ops | norm² outside | Z_r/Z_G | ΔA₀(GELT − P) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| published (4 levels) | 4 | 21.1% | 0.229 | +0.157 ± 0.027 (5.8σ) |
| deep (7 levels) | 7 | 12.8% | 0.146 | +0.097 ± 0.021 (4.5σ) |
| full (7lv + shapes) | 21 | **2.5%** | 0.013 | +0.021 ± 0.009 (2.3σ) |

**What is the out-of-span content made of?** Projecting `r` onto the richer
`span{full}` answers it: **79.6 ± 0.3%** of it lands inside — i.e. it is mostly
*rectangular loops the network rediscovered*, without ever being shown one. The
remaining ~2.5% of the operator's norm² is expressed by no operator in the
21-member classical basis, and it still carries ΔA₀ = +0.021 ± 0.009.

### The control that was missing, and what it changed

![Trained vs untrained decomposition](update_2026-09-18/decomposition_trained_vs_untrained.png)

Six untrained 7-level networks (3 init seeds × 2 ensembles, evaluated only) were
put through the identical pipeline. The result is a genuine correction to how
this study should be read:

1. **Being outside the classical span is architectural, not learned.** Untrained
   nets are 16%, 31% and 74% outside — *further* than the trained one's 12.8%.
   Training moves the operator **closer** to the classical span while making it
   better. The norm fraction alone therefore proves nothing.
2. **The discriminating statistic is Z_r/Z_G** — the spectral weight the
   residual actually carries: 0.146 trained against 0.047 untrained, i.e. 0.41
   against 0.10 once normalised per unit of out-of-span norm.
3. **And the sharpest one: `r` is loop shapes only when the network is trained.**
   80% of the trained residual is reachable inside `span{full}`; **0.3%** of the
   untrained residual is. Training rotates the out-of-span content onto the part
   of operator space that Wilson loops span, then keeps a small piece that is not.
4. **The sign of ΔA₀(net − P) flips**: deleting `r` costs the trained network its
   whole advantage (+0.097 ± 0.021) and *helps* an untrained one
   (−0.091 ± 0.042) — its own classical projection is the better operator.

## 5. Given parity, where *can* attention win?

This is the strategic question §1 opens, and it now has a running record with
two closed attempts and one measurement.

### 5.1 The statement of the difference had to be repaired twice

The loose framing "an L-CNN is a polynomial, GELT is not" is **wrong**: `LAct` is
`g(Re Tr W/n_c)·W`, i.e. input-dependent gating, and L-Conv mixes channels before
it — so an L-CNN can realise a data-dependent difference test between scales, and
GELT carries the *same* L-Act. The correct statement is narrower:

> The only *mechanism* difference is whether the weights over **offsets** are
> input-dependent. Gating, bilinearity, receptive field, loop degree, inputs,
> loss, splits and estimator are matched by construction.

Two candidate mechanisms follow, and they are not the same bet: **(M1)** relative
weighting — softmax depends only on score *differences*, so α is a scale-free
ratio recomputed per site per configuration; **(M2)** boundedness — the convex
combination of §1's robustness result.

Then the second repair, which is the important one: GELT and the matched L-CNN
differ in **two** things, M1 *and* transport geometry (shortest-path-averaged
L1-ball vs axis-aligned link products). **So no GELT-vs-L-CNN number in this
project has ever measured M1** — a tie is not evidence against it and a win would
not have been evidence for it. The clean test has to be an *ablation*.

### 5.2 One attempt stopped by a four-hour pre-flight instead of a 60-hour run

The candidate task was flow-free topological charge density (predict `q_x` at
Wilson-flow time t from unflowed links). Everything built for it worked — the
clover charge is now exactly parity-odd to 1.4e-17 site by site, and is in the
test suite. It was stopped on a measurement, before any training code ran:

| rung | topologically meaningful? | best classical R² | classical **at the network's own reach** |
|---|---|---|---|
| t = 1 | no | 0.891 (48 APE steps) | **0.876** — 98% of the headroom gone |
| t = 4 | **yes** | **0.978** | **0.149** |

There is no rung where the target is topologically meaningful, the receptive
field is adequate, *and* headroom exists over free classical cooling. Two
durable lessons came out of it: **never define a target as the output of a
classical smoother the baseline can also run** (the smoother is then the ceiling,
and it has effectively unbounded reach), and **measure the best classical method
at the architecture's own reach before building any training code**. Cost of
reaching that verdict: ~4 h of V100 against the ≥60–100 GPU-hours the full study
would have taken. A free by-product: **APE cooling reproduces the Wilson flow
exactly** (one α = 0.5 step ↔ flow ε = 0.0833, confirmed to three decimals at two
flow times) at ~8% of the cost.

### 5.3 The M1 ablation — and M1 pays

The clean test: **GELT against GELT with the softmax frozen** — α becomes a
learned per-offset table, same transport, same bilinear value path, same
parameters. It is still a softmax, so it removes input-dependence (M1) while
keeping boundedness (M2) fixed. Three constructed per-site targets of increasing
demand, a pre-registered readings list, 5 arms × 3 targets × 3 seeds × 2
ensembles + controls = **132 runs**.

![M1 probe](update_2026-09-18/m1_probe.png)

| reading | median ΔR² (T2) | sign | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-A calibration, `gelt` − `lcnn` on T0 | +0.013 | 3/6 | 1.000 — passes |
| **R-B `gelt` − `frozen`** | **+0.144** | **6/6** | **0.031** |
| R-B′ `gelt` − `frozen_matched` (capacity control) | +0.162 | 5/6 | 0.219 |
| R-C `gelt` − `lcnn` | +0.348 | 4/6 | 0.688 |
| R-D `lcnn_norm` − `lcnn` (the M2 control) | −0.001 | 3/6 | 1.000 |

1. **M1 pays — the first time in three attempts that it has been observed to.**
   Positive in all six paired cells; p = 0.031 is the floor a sign test reaches
   at n = 6. The capacity-matched frozen arm (94% of GELT's parameters) is no
   closer, so it is the mechanism and not the budget.
2. **Dispersion was the unplanned result**: sd over the six runs is 0.049 for
   GELT against 0.415 for the matched L-CNN — a 70× variance ratio. All six GELT
   runs exceed R² = 0.81; three of six L-CNN runs fall below 0.25.
3. **But part of that consistency is the transport, not the attention.** Feeding
   the *same* network a single-path transport instead of the averaged one leaves
   accuracy unchanged (R-I = −0.037, 2/6) and costs it its consistency: `gelt`
   never falls below R² = 0.70 in 12 cells, the single-path variants do twice and
   three times. **This is a claim elsewhere in the thesis that needs re-wording:
   GELT's robustness is attention *and* the transport, not attention alone.**
4. **Path averaging buys no accuracy on this target** — and the transport is
   62.8% of a GELT step, so the 3.9× cost gap against the L-CNN is a *choice*
   here, not a necessity.
5. The M2 case is **weaker** than it looked: its dedicated control is a null on
   accuracy and only 2.15× on dispersion, and point 3 shows the same
   failure-count signature appearing with α held completely fixed.

**Limits, stated plainly:** the targets are constructed, not physics — this is a
mechanism assay, and it knowingly violates the "the task must be a physics task"
criterion the selection rule otherwise imposes. n = 6, so p = 0.031 is the
ceiling on significance. One β, one volume, one training budget. **M1 has still
never been observed to pay on a physics task.**

> **That last sentence still stands, and §§8–9 are the two attempts since that
> tried to overturn it.** Both are null. The proposal §5 ends on — vortex
> geometry in 3D Z₂ — ran, and is §8.

## 6. Performance, briefly

Four exactly-equivalent optimisations landed, each measured and each covered by a
test: SU(2) projection in closed form (the APE ladder was issuing ~4.5 M batched
2×2 complex SVDs per step), APE smearing vectorised over the configuration batch
(7.4×), introspection made opt-in, and the attention hot path (2.16× on forward +
backward, outputs identical to 1.2e-7). **End to end on the V100: 7.77 → 5.04
s/step, i.e. 1.54× in situ.**

The step is now fully accounted for per stage, which re-ranked the backlog: the
**transport is 62.8% of the step**, and the adjoint SO(3) representation would
take it 4930 → 2720 ms, **1.8× end to end**. Everything else is a few percent.
(One earlier profiling conclusion — "rope_score is the largest single stage at
238 ms" — was an artifact of the micro-bench and has been retracted in place.)

## 7. One caveat that touches a published table

**Z₂ APE smearing is not gauge covariant at the production α = 0.5.** With two
spatial staples the update is `V = (1−α)U + (α/2)(s₁+s₂)`, which at exactly
α = ½ gives `V = 0` whenever the staples disagree with the link, and `Z2.project`
sends `0 → +1` — a value that does not transform. Measured on production
configurations: the ladder freezes after one step, 99.9% of changed links are
gauge-dependent tie-breaks, and Ō(t) moves 1.82σ under a gauge transformation.

Consequences, and their limits: the Z₂ classical comparator in the attention
table is **one operator, not four**, and the Z₂ networks were trained on four
input channels of which three are byte-identical. **SU(2) is verified clean**
(1.4e-15), so every spectroscopy number and the SU(2) attention row are
untouched. The fix is α = 0.7 or unprojected fat links, and it needs a retrain
for a clean end-to-end statement.

## 8. Attempt 4 — vortex-cluster geometry in 3D Z₂: a tie, and a retraction

§5 closed with *"M1 has still never been observed to pay on a physics task"*.
This is the attempt to change that, and the first candidate that is a physics
observable **and** clears all four selection criteria. It ran 19–20 September
and it is closed.

**The task.** On a 48 × 24 × 24 Z₂ lattice the negative plaquettes are dual
links, and Bianchi closes them into loops — centre vortices. The target is
per-site: `V1 = log(1 + size of the largest vortex cluster touching x)`. It is
global connectivity read locally, which is exactly "which distant structure
matters here", and no classical *local* algorithm computes it — criterion 4 is
satisfied without argument.

**The pre-flight ran first and cost four hours.** Five gates on four cached
ensembles, all passing, plus the two classical ceilings the rule demands. The
one that matters is the bar a four-layer network can actually be asked to
clear: a BFS on the dual graph, truncated to the same ball **and to the same
number of message-passing rounds**. That second truncation was missed on the
first pass and is the correction the study turned on — an uncapped BFS follows a
line for as many steps as the line is long, so comparing against it measures
depth, not inductive bias.

| β | linear filter (the M1-free ceiling) | **BFS, 4 hops — matched depth** | BFS, unlimited |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.7450 | +0.106 | **+0.450** | +0.747 |
| **0.7520** (primary) | +0.152 | **+0.404** | +0.671 |
| 0.7560 | +0.156 | **+0.515** | +0.806 |
| 0.7585 | +0.280 | **+0.500** | +0.762 |

A measured tension the ladder exposes and the design had to absorb: **the
M1-free linear ceiling rises towards β_c** (0.106 → 0.280), so the physics is
most interesting where the architecture question is least clean. The primary
coupling was fixed at 0.7520 for that reason, before any training.

**The result — no difference.** Six initialisation seeds per arm, each arm at
its own bracketed learning rate (GELT 3e-3, the L-CNN 3e-4 — an order of
magnitude apart, which is why they had to be bracketed separately; running the
baseline at GELT's rate diverges it, and reading that as a defeat would have
been the straw man §2 already had to audit once):

> **ΔR²(GELT − L-CNN) = −0.073 ± 0.051 on V1 at β = 0.7520, t = 1.4, p ≈ 0.21.**

**The one unambiguously positive statement** is W-E: both architectures clear
the matched-depth classical bar, mean 0.614 and 0.542 against +0.404. A learned
equivariant operator beats the best bounded-reach classical algorithm on a
physics observable — which is what the pre-flight rule was built to test and
what the topology attempt of §5.2 failed.

**And the reading that is significant goes against GELT.** Its spread over
initialisations is **6.2× the L-CNN's** (sd 0.123 against 0.020, F = 38 on
(5,5) df, p < 0.002), falling below the classical bar in 3 of 6 seeds against
0 of 6. That **reverses §1's robustness result** — the last asymmetry still
favouring GELT — so that claim had to be re-stated as task-dependent everywhere
it appears. §9 then took it further.

**Three methodological findings, each of which changed a number:**

1. **The training budget was the binding constraint, not the learning rate.** At
   120 epochs no arm had converged and the *sign of M1 was inverted*; the
   ranking at that horizon was an artefact of the budget. A cosine annealed to
   `T_max = EPOCHS` makes every horizon a different experiment, so "still
   improving" is not comparable across horizons.
2. **Gradient steps, not epochs.** 70 training configurations at batch 4 is 18
   steps per epoch. Batch 1 gives 70 at the same wall clock and moved every
   reading by more than any learning rate did — one arm by 0.17.
3. **The silent-variation defect, found twice more.** Neither the batch size nor
   the L-CNN's initialisation scale was in the dumps, the artifact names or the
   overwrite guard, so two runs differing in them were indistinguishable in
   their outputs. Both are recorded now. This is the third instance of the same
   class of defect in this project.

**One repair, stated and applied symmetrically.** One seed per arm returned an
R² of −66.8 and −259.2 with error bars the size of the value and healthy
validation curves. The per-configuration sums in every dump say each is **one
held-out configuration** — different ones for the two arms — so it is a rare
initialisation × configuration interaction, it happens once in six to *both*
architectures, and R²'s unbounded tail converts it into a number that swamps the
statistic. The table uses each run's leave-one-out value.

<a name="9"></a>
## 9. Attempt 5 — β-transfer: a null that retracted §8's one significant reading

Four attempts, four ties. The tempting reading is "attention has no advantage";
the record supports something narrower and more useful, which is a property of
the *benchmarks*:

> Every A/B in this project trains **and tests at one coupling, one volume, one
> input distribution**, with each arm at its own tuned rate, and scores a mean
> over a well-behaved test set.

That is the right design for an accuracy comparison — it is what §2's audit
forced — and it is also precisely the setting in which a softmax that
renormalises the weighting over offsets *per configuration* has nothing to earn.
So this attempt is not a new task. It is §8's task, §8's own frozen checkpoints,
evaluated at the three other cached couplings. **Nothing is trained**: forward
passes, about an hour, on work already done.

Two readings per checkpoint. *Raw* applies the operator as it stands, mapped
back to physical units through its own run's standardisation. *Affine* allows a
two-parameter recalibration fitted on the new coupling's training split,
identical for both arms — the kinder reading for the arm with a scale problem,
and therefore the one on which an advantage is about **routing** rather than
amplitude. The statistic is a **difference of differences**, each seed its own
control, because §8's 6.2× seed spread is an order of magnitude above either
arm's jackknife error.

**The result — null.** The contrast favours GELT at **3 of 3** couplings
(+0.007, +0.310, +0.013) and p is 0.485, 0.240, 0.240. The claim was
pre-registered as requiring p < 0.05 at at least one; it is not made.

**What it retracted.** §8's dispersion result is a property of the training
coupling, not of the architectures:

| β | sd(GELT) | sd(L-CNN) | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0.7520** (where both were trained) | 0.122 | 0.020 | **6.2× against GELT** |
| 0.7450 | 0.113 | 0.075 | 1.5× |
| 0.7560 | 0.270 | 0.348 | 1.3×, *the other way* |
| 0.7585 | 0.264 | 0.256 | 1.0× |

GELT's spread barely moves across all four; the **L-CNN's is created by the
shift**, rising 17× at 0.7560. So §8's sentence is right about the task and
incomplete about the coupling, and every quotation of the 6.2× now carries both.

**The finding neither architecture can spin, and the most useful one here:
transfer failure is invisible in distribution.** Off-coupling the runs are
bimodal — a seed either holds its R² to within a few hundredths or falls to
≈ 0 — and the collapsing runs cannot be identified from the coupling they were
trained on. GELT's one collapsing seed is its **second best** operator at the
training coupling; the L-CNN's three are ranked 3rd, 5th and 6th; the L-CNN's
best seed transfers fine. **Selecting on validation loss does not select a
transferable operator**, for either architecture. For a scan across couplings
that is a practical statement independent of which one wins.

**What survives of "robustness", stated as narrowly as the evidence allows.**
Not an error-bar advantage (§1 measured parity at every Δ), not an architectural
property (§8 reversed it), not a task property (§9 shows the reversal is
coupling-specific). What is left is a **failure-rate** asymmetry that appears in
the predicted direction whenever the input distribution is stressed:

| observation | GELT | L-CNN |
|---|---|---|
| SU(2) 0⁺⁺, share of C(0) on one configuration (§1) | 0 of 26 | 3 of 13 |
| M1 probe, T2 (§5.3) | 6 of 6 above R² 0.81 | 3 of 6 below 0.25 |
| β-transfer, seeds losing > 0.2 of R² | 1 of 6 | 3 of 6 |

Three times directional, **significant none of them** — the third row is Fisher
p = 0.545, its threshold was chosen after the fact, and the study's own
pre-existing criterion (cells below the matched-depth bar) reads 6 of 18 for
each arm. This is the point: the claim has been asserted three times on counts
and never once measured. The designed experiment that would settle it exists on
paper — stress the input distribution deliberately and read *failure rate* as
the primary statistic — and attempt 5 has now validated its most expensive
stressor for free, since coupling transfer demonstrably produces the failures.
What it lacks is statistics, not a mechanism.

<a name="10"></a>
## 10. The baseline itself: ours is a restriction of the authors' L-CNN

Every GELT-vs-L-CNN number in this project — §1's parity included — was produced
against **our reimplementation** of the L-CNN, not against the authors' code.
That was always known and is written in the benchmark script's own docstring;
what was not known is how much it costs. It is now measured, and constructively.

Our block factors the bilinear kernel through the channel axis (an L-Conv then
an L-Bilin); theirs is one merged kernel, unrestricted in the (local,
transported) index pair. So **ours is a low-rank member of their family**, and
the containment is exact: given a random kernel of theirs, ω can be set to
select the transported basis one direction per channel and β to their kernel
verbatim, and the two layers agree to **1.9e-15**. The correspondence between
their column ordering and ours is *discovered* by matching the two bases
numerically, so the test cannot pass by re-implementing their convention and
checking itself against it.

The width at which the two coincide is `c_out = 2·c_in·(1 + 2DK)`. **The
production arms run far below it:**

| task | our width | width for full expressiveness | fraction |
|---|---|---|---|
| M1 probe (3 input channels) | 6 | 78 | **8%** |
| glueball (12 input channels) | 5 | 312 | **1.6%** |

So the difference is **expressiveness, not parametrisation**, and it is two
orders of magnitude in reachable kernel directions at the widths the thesis
actually ran. That is a reason to expect the re-runs to move, and it is the
first question an examiner asks about a baseline — "we reimplemented it" is not
the answer that closes it.

**Status: built and tested, nothing measured.** The authors' layers are wrapped
verbatim out of the vendored source, their activation included (taking their
layer and keeping our activation would make "the authors' L-CNN" mean nothing),
and `lcnn_ref` is an arm wherever `lcnn` is. Three traps are each now a test —
their `kernel_size` is our `K + 1`, so passing K would silently halve the
baseline's receptive field per layer with every other check still passing; their
layout packs the links into the field tensor; their layers are complex
throughout. One asymmetry is *not* quantified: their weights are real and ours
complex, so the inclusion is proved in one direction only.

## 11. What I would like to discuss

**The list has changed since 18 September, and item 1 is the reason.**

1. **§10 is the one that cannot wait.** Every comparison in §1 — the parity
   result the thesis is built on — is against our reimplementation, which is now
   *proved* to be a low-rank restriction running at 8% and 1.6% of the width at
   which it equals the published layer. Three options, and I do not think the
   first is defensible: (a) report parity as it stands and document the
   restriction; (b) re-run §1 against the authors' block, which is built and
   tested and costs one night; (c) re-run and report both. My reading is (b)
   at minimum, because the restriction cuts the *baseline's* expressiveness,
   so a tie against it is the weaker of the two possible ties. **What is the
   supervisor's read on how much of §1 has to be re-run?**
2. **What the thesis now claims attention buys, given that robustness is
   gone.** §1's list was: the L1-ball reach within a layer, an attention field
   that is itself a measurable lattice operator, robustness across training
   runs — at 3.9× the step cost. §8 reversed the robustness item and §9 showed
   the reversal is itself coupling-specific, so the honest list is **two items
   and a cost**, plus a failure-rate asymmetry that has been observed three
   times and measured zero. Is two items and a cost a thesis-sized claim?
3. **Whether the negative result is a chapter.** Five attempts to find a task
   where attention beats a matched L-CNN: two stopped on pre-flights, three run
   to completion, all null on the architecture question. Written up properly
   that is a *selection rule* — four criteria plus a pre-flight that has twice
   stopped a study for hours instead of weeks — and the strongest positive
   statement inside it is §8's, that **both** learned architectures beat the
   best bounded-reach classical algorithm on a physics observable. I think this
   is a real chapter and not an apology, but it is a judgement call about what
   the thesis is *for*.
4. **Whether to spend GPU time on the Z₂ smearing fix** (§7) for a clean Z₂
   attention table, or to leave it as a documented caveat and put the hours into
   the SU(2) side. Unchanged since 18 September, and now competing with item 1
   for the same nights.
5. ~~**Whether the M1 result is worth a physics task.**~~ **Answered, twice,
   both negative** (§8, §9). It remains a mechanism assay on constructed
   targets, and the recommendation that follows is to stop looking for the
   physics task and run the *designed* experiment instead: stress the input
   distribution and read **failure rate** as the primary statistic, which is
   the one thing three separate studies have pointed at and none has measured.
   §9 validated its most expensive stressor by accident.
