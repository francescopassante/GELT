## 2. The confirmed defect: Z₂ APE smearing

The retired `z2_fair_fight.py`'s docstring records a pre-flight finding from
2026-08-17.
This audit re-derived it independently, on production configurations, and it is
**worse than the pre-flight stated**.

### 2.1 The mechanism

In 3D with spatial-only smearing there are two staples, so
`V = (1−α)U + (α/2)(s₁ + s₂)` with `U, sᵢ ∈ {±1}`:

    s₁ + s₂ =  0  →  V = (1−α)U        sign of U preserved, always
    s₁ + s₂ = ±2  →  V = ±α + (1−α)U   the staples win iff α > ½

So projected Z₂ APE smearing has **no tunable radius at any α**: it is the exact
identity below ½ and a majority-vote cellular automaton above ½, which reaches a
fixed point in a few sweeps. At α = ½ exactly, `V = 0` in precisely the cases
where the staples disagree with the link, and `Z2.project` sends `0 → +1` by its
`>= 0` rule — a value that does **not** transform, since covariance demands
`project(ω·0·ω′) = ω·project(0)·ω′`, which fails whenever `ωω′ = −1`.

`SMEAR_ALPHA = 0.5` is the production value in both `train_z2_glueball.py` and
`train_glueball.py`.

### 2.3 Blast radius

**SU(2) is clean.** Checked, not assumed: `SU.project` is the SVD polar factor,
continuous, no ties. Covariance violation **1.4e-15** at α = 0.5 and 6.1e-15 at
α = 0.7, and the ladder keeps moving (mean |ΔU| = 0.197, 0.087, 0.056, 0.042).
So `su2_fair_fight.py`'s prediction that the Z₂ defects are Z₂-specific is
**confirmed**, and the §6.2 headline — the thesis's core claim — is untouched.

**Z₂ is affected in three places.**

1. **The classical comparator is one operator, not four.** The ladder freezes
   after one step, so `SMEAR_LEVELS = [0, 4, 8, 16]` holds two distinct
   operators. The saved dump already recorded the consequence and it was never
   connected to the cause — `results/attention/z2_attention_correlator_diag_R6.pt`
   has `n_basis=4, n_ops=1, gevp_fell_back=True` **at all five β**. The paper's
   "optimal combination of a four-level smeared Morningstar–Peardon basis" is, in
   the Z₂ section, a single smeared operator at every coupling.

2. **The network's own inputs are degenerate and slightly gauge-variant.**
   `train_z2_glueball.py` sets `INPUT_SMEAR_LEVELS = (0, 4, 8, 16)`, and
   `z2_attention_correlator.py` overrides it only in smoke mode — so the Z₂ nets
   were trained *and* evaluated on four channels of which **three are
   byte-identical**, one of which carries 0.67% gauge-variant plaquettes. The
   abstract's "exact invariance of the network output is verified to machine
   precision" is true of the **architecture** (and `check_gelt_invariance.py`
   tests exactly that) but is **not** true end-to-end of the Z₂ input pipeline.
   This needs one sentence in the paper regardless of what the fair fight finds.

3. **The dual accuracy table is measured against a handicapped opponent.**
   The dual ground truth (retired; see `cfa0a7e`) reported classical −8.9%, attention-trained
   +3.8%, attention-random −8.0%. Gauge noise on an operator lowers A₀ and biases
   ξ **low** — the same direction as the classical deficit. This does not mean
   the result is wrong; the random-network arm shares the same inputs and lands
   at −8.0% without using the classical basis at all, which is a point in the
   result's favour. It does mean the comparison is not clean, and it is the first
   thing a referee will attack.

### 2.4 Why the tests missed it

`tests/test_glueball.py`'s covariance cases use α = 0.7 and α = 0.6 — never the
production α = 0.5. Adding that case is a two-line change and it will **fail**,
which is the point. It was deliberately *not* added during this audit: the
paper's acknowledgments cite the suite passing in full (111 tests), so turning
the suite red is a call for the author to make alongside the fix, not a
drive-by.

## 3. The new result

`notes/operator_decomposition.md` + `scripts/operator_decomposition.py`, written
during this audit. Short version: the learned operator has a component
**12.9% ± 0.4% / 11.7% ± 0.3%** (two independent ensembles) of its norm² outside
the span of the entire classical basis; that component carries 13–14% of the
ground-state amplitude; removing it costs the whole advantage
(**ΔA₀ = +0.076 ± 0.019, 4.0σ** combined, at unchanged mass); and it is not a
contact term (the fraction *grows* under the C(τ) metrics, 0.129 → 0.156 →
0.197). The classical ladder's own increments — 0.689 → 0.197 → 0.049, a
converged geometric series replicating to the third decimal across ensembles —
supply the scale that makes 13% meaningful.

It reproduces the published GELT-vs-GEVP numbers exactly as a gate. Its two
missing controls and its one falsifiable prediction about the fair fight are in
that note's §5 and §6.

## 6. The fair fights, run (2026-09-07)

Both scripts ran on the V100. Three defects had to be fixed to get there, all
recorded in the git history of that day: a CPU-built Ω in the covariance
self-check, the Z₂ script differencing ΔA₀ only against the pre-registered arm
(irrecoverable offline — the summary dump carries no per-config Ō, which is now
kept), and the SU(2) script projecting through `fit_glueball_overlap.
project_ground` at the library default `eps = 1e-12`.

That last one produced a **false positive first**, and it is the cautionary tale
of this audit: the strengthened arms came back *worse* than the basis they
contain — `full` (21 ops) at A₀ = 0.072 against `published`'s 0.837, `deep` at
0.156 ± 1.007 — and the script duly reported "the claim survives its strongest
opponent, 8.8σ". `published` is a strict **subset** of both, so their GEVP
optimises over a larger span and cannot interpolate worse. The estimator had
selected a near-null direction of an ill-conditioned C(t0); the win was noise,
and it pointed the flattering way. `z2_attention_correlator.py` had already
solved this (GEVP_EPS = 1e-4 + a variational fallback); the SU(2) script now
carries the same guards, prints cond C(t0) per arm, and refuses a verdict
outright when the superset property fails.

### 6.4 Matched inputs (2026-09-08) — parity, and a saturated observable

§6.1's answer was "a deeper smearing ladder beats GELT", not "classical beats
GELT": `deep`/`full` used levels 8–16, while the net's inputs were
`INPUT_SMEAR_LEVELS = (0,2,4,6)`. Loop *shapes* are fair game for the classical
side (depth builds larger loops from plaquettes), but iterated smearing is not
(Run 4). So the net was retrained on the matching ladder —
`--input-smear-levels=0,2,4,6,8,12,16 --d-model=24`, 21 input channels, wider
residual stream, one training from scratch on the Run-5 ensemble.

    arm          A₀ (run5)      ΔA₀ (GELT − arm)
    published    0.837(56)      +0.118 ± 0.045  (2.6σ)
    deep         0.903(62)      +0.052 ± 0.033  (1.5σ)
    shapes       0.500(110)     +0.455 ± 0.114  (4.0σ)
    shapes_sm    0.934(67)      +0.021 ± 0.053  (0.4σ)
    full         0.942(54)      +0.013 ± 0.029  (0.5σ)
    gelt         0.955(57)          — (was 0.903(47) on the 4-level ladder)

**The net improved with the deeper inputs** (0.903 → 0.955), by about what the
classical side gained from the same levels — the Run-4 mechanism, confirmed from
the other direction: the content is unreachable by depth and has to be fed.

**At matched inputs neither wins.** +0.013 ± 0.029 against a 21-operator basis.
The claim that survives is about *economy*: one learned operator equals the
optimal linear combination of 21 classical ones built from the same information.
The overlap claim of §6.2 as written does not survive, and both strengthenings
(deeper ladder, more shapes) close it independently — `deep` and `shapes_sm`
each land within 1.5σ on their own.

**Two limits on this number.** (i) One ensemble: ±0.029 cannot resolve a
difference below ≈ 6% in A₀ at 2σ, so "parity" means "no difference larger than
that". The `_ens1` twin was never trained on this ladder; doing so would take the
error to ≈ ±0.02. (ii) **A₀ has saturated.** Every strong arm is at 0.93–0.96
against a ceiling of 1 (Run 5's ens1 read 1.013, a fit artifact of a nearly pure
operator). On this ensemble and channel the ground-state overlap no longer has
the dynamic range to discriminate methods — a harder test (excited states, a
different channel, a coarser/larger lattice, or a cost-matched comparison) is
needed for any future claim of the form "A beats B".

**Caveat on the model.** `d_model` 16 → 24, so this net is wider than Run 5 and a
GELT-vs-GELT reading confounds reach with capacity. Against the classical arms
that is irrelevant — a GEVP basis has no parameters to match. The
capacity-matched alternative (a 4-level ladder reaching the same depth,
`0,4,8,16`, 12 channels) was not run.

### 6.5 The ens1 replication (2026-09-09) — the claim survives, against the arm that can be trusted

`ens1` was retrained on the same deep ladder (`--input-smear-levels=0,2,4,6,8,12,16
--d-model=24`) and the fight re-run on both dumps together. Every arm's
condition number and variational-gate status is now printed per ensemble —
which is what saves this run from repeating §6.1's false positive in the other
direction.

    arm          n_ops   cond C(t0)         gate (run5 / ens1)
    published        4   2.4e3   / 2.4e3    ok / ok
    deep             7   1.9e6   / 7.0e6    ok / ok
    shapes           5   85      / 87       ok / FELL BACK (member 1)
    shapes_sm       20   5.6e10  / 2.8e9    ok / FELL BACK (member 19)
    full            21   inf     / inf      ok / ok

**`full` and `shapes_sm` are not trustworthy verdicts, not unfavorable ones.**
`full`'s C(t0) is numerically singular on *both* ensembles (cond = ∞): the
gate's "ok" only means the projection is no worse than its best single member,
not that the whitening resolved a real direction — the eigenvector is fixed by
the `GEVP_EPS` floor, not by signal. Consistent with that: `full`'s ΔA₀ is
**not reproducible** between ensembles (+0.013 ± 0.029 vs +0.105 ± 0.056, an
8× swing in the central value), so the "combined 1.3σ" reported by the script
is an average of noise, not a measurement. `shapes_sm` is worse: on ens1 it
outright fell back to a single operator (cond 2.8e9 pushed it past the
variational gate), so its two ensemble numbers do not even measure the same
estimator — one is a 20-op GEVP, the other a single smeared-shaped plaquette.

**`deep` is the one strong classical arm that is clean on both ensembles** —
cond ~1.9e6/7.0e6, gate never fires, same estimator both times. It is also the
correct input-matched comparator (levels 0,2,4,6,8,12,16 × the 1×1 plaquette,
exactly the net's own channels). Its ΔA₀: +0.052 ± 0.033 (run5),
+0.127 ± 0.027 (ens1), **combined +0.097 ± 0.021 (4.6σ)**. `published`
(cond ~2.4e3, always clean) combines to +0.131 ± 0.029 (4.5σ).

**Verdict: §6.2's ΔA₀ survives at matched inputs, against the classical arm
that can actually be trusted (`deep`).** §6.4's "does not survive" was measured
on run5 alone, against `full` — an arm that was already suspect there (this
note's §6.1 false positive was the same failure mode, `full` at cond-driven
noise) and is now confirmed unusable on both ensembles. §6.4's central claim
("economy, not overlap") is withdrawn; §6.2 as written — the learned operator
carries more ground-state weight than the best classical combination — holds
at matched smearing depth.

**What is still open, not closed.** `full` and `shapes_sm` were meant to be the
sharpest opponents (most loop shapes, most levels) and neither can be read.
Fixing them needs pruning near-degenerate operators from the 20/21-op tables
before the GEVP, not just raising `GEVP_EPS` further — a bigger floor would
hide the same instability rather than resolve it. Until that is done, "does a
classical basis with genuinely more loop shapes than `deep` close the gap" is
**unanswered**, not answered negative. `deep` alone does not exercise loop
geometry at all (single shape, matched depth only).
